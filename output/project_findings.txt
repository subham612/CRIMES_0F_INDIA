CRIME ANALYSIS OF INDIA (2014–2016)
=============================================

Number of analyzed regions: 36
States: 29
Union Territories: 7

TOTAL RECORDED CRIMES BY YEAR
2014: 2,851,563 (baseline year)
2015: 2,949,400 (+97,837; +3.43% vs 2014)
2016: 2,975,711 (+26,311; +0.89% vs 2015)

2016 CRIME-RATE DISTRIBUTION BY REGION CATEGORY (per 100,000 population)
State: n=29, median=192.3, IQR=128.7–237.2
UT: n=7, median=144.8, IQR=69.2–204.6

KEY FINDINGS
Highest 2016 crime count: Uttar Pradesh (282,171)
Highest 2016 recorded crime rate: Delhi (974.9)
Largest increase, 2014–2016: Delhi (+53,865; +34.61%)
Largest decrease, 2014–2016: Rajasthan (-30,020; -14.27%)
Population/crime-rate Pearson correlation: 0.075; this is a very weak linear association in these 36 regions, not evidence of causation.
2016 rate values beyond the 1.5-IQR fences (-47.9–410.5): Delhi (974.9), Kerala (727.6). These are statistical flags for context, not evidence of incorrect data.

METHODOLOGY
Pandas was used for loading, cleaning, summarizing, and exporting. NumPy was used for safe percentage-change calculations. Matplotlib was used for visualizations.

LIMITATIONS
Recorded IPC crimes do not necessarily represent all crimes committed. Reporting practices, population differences, and the small number of years affect interpretation. State/UT group summaries describe distributions of regions and are not population-weighted. Correlation does not establish causation. Crime-rate outliers are statistical observations, not proof of incorrect data.
